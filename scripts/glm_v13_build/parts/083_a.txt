[1] Title: Civic Centre Design Competition Announced, Day: February 5, 1966 Content: The borough council has announced an open design competition for the new civic centre, entries to be received by the summer. The competition, formally opened on 4 February 1966, follows the site clearance that began with the first demolition contract on 1 March 1965 and ran until the last foundations were grubbed out this January. Some three hundred firms are expected to enter. The assessors' panel includes the county architect and two outside members. Meanwhile the temporary exhibition of the clearance plans, mounted on 12 April 1966 in the old library hall, will close on 30 September 1966, having been extended twice by public demand. The council's leader said the competition marks the borough's coming of age. A public inquiry into the compulsory purchase of the remaining strips is fixed to open on 9 January 1967, and its report is expected by early summer.
[2] Title: Civic Centre Plan Approved at Last, Day: November 28, 1968 Content: The Secretary of State has approved the civic centre plans, the decision landing on 27 November 1968, two years and nine months after the design competition opened its envelopes on 4 February 1966. The approved scheme retains the plaza and the twin blocks but loses the proposed conference hall, which will be reconsidered at a later stage. The public inquiry of January 1967, whose report was published on 22 May 1967, had been the chief obstacle, objectors pressing the loss of light to the schoolrooms. The council now moves to contract documents, and a start on site is projected for early summer next year. The exhibition of the approved scheme, successor to the 1966 show, opens in the same library hall from Monday. The assessors' winning firm has been retained for the working drawings. The leader paid tribute to the objectors, whose case had improved the scheme's landscaping.
[3] Title: New Civic Centre Opens Its Doors, Day: March 15, 1972 Content: The civic centre opened by the mayor on 14 March 1972 completes a build that began with the first site huts on 2 June 1969, thirty-four months of construction through two hard winters. The opening ceremony, held in the plaza, was followed by a civic service and a public reception. Landscaping of the eastern approach remains to be finished, the contract for which runs to 30 January 1973. The building houses the register office, the magistrates' courtrooms, and the borough's offices under one roof for the first time. The contractor's programme, disrupted by the 1970 brickmakers' strike, was recovered by double-shift working in the final year. The total cost stands at four and a quarter million pounds against an estimate of three and nine. The conference hall, lost at the 1968 approval, is promised for a future stage, no date being set.

[1] Title: Market Hall Feasibility Study Begun, Day: March 21, 1974 Content: The harbour commissioners have commissioned a feasibility study for a covered market hall on the quayside, the study set in train on 20 March 1974. The study, by consultants, will examine trading patterns, footfall, and the state of the old fish cellar vaults. A committee of commissioners and traders will receive the report in the autumn, its findings then to guide a formal application for board approval. The hall, if approved, would house the fish market and the weekly general market under one roof, with cold stores cut into the vaults. Traders have given the idea a mixed welcome, the fish merchants warm and the general stallholders wary of rents. The commissioners' chairman said the quayside must earn its keep or lose its grant. The study's cost, eight thousand pounds, is shared with the county.
[2] Title: Market Hall Topped Out, Day: March 2, 1977 Content: The harbour market hall was topped out on 28 February 1977, ten months after work began on site on 6 April 1976 following the board's acceptance of the consultants' report. The committee's report, delivered on 15 October 1974, had recommended the covered hall and the vault stores, and the intervening months were spent on plans and consents. The topping-out ceremony, performed by the senior market porter, placed the last roof tile. Fitting-out now proceeds, and the hall's opening is fixed for early December. The vault stores were cut ahead of programme, their arches bricked by the last of the harbour's own masons. The contractor confirmed the budget holds at half a million pounds. A stall ballot for the new floor will be held in the new year.
[3] Title: Market Hall Piazza Finished, Day: November 27, 1980 Content: The piazza in front of the harbour market hall was finished on 26 November 1980, the last element of the scheme begun with the hall's topping out in 1977. The hall itself had opened on 9 December 1977, and the stall ballot followed, running from 3 May 1978 to the allocations of 21 July 1978. The piazza works, begun on 14 January 1980, repaved the old fish quay in granite setts and installed the water features whose pumps have already needed replacement once. The commissioners declared the whole undertaking complete and within budget. The hall's first trading year exceeded the consultants' footfall forecast by a fifth, and the vault stores are fully let. The chairman said the study of 1974 had proved its eight thousand pounds many times over. Winter lighting for the piazza is to be added from next year's maintenance fund.

[1] Title: Campaign Launched for New Swimming Baths, Day: February 3, 1983 Content: A public campaign for the replacement of the town's Edwardian swimming baths was launched on 2 February 1983 at a meeting in the temperance hall attended by three hundred people. The campaign, backed by the swimming club, the schools, and the trades council, asks the district council to include a new pool in its capital programme. The old baths, built in 1908, have failed two structural surveys, and their boiler is on its last legs. The campaign's petition will be presented at the council's next meeting, and spokesmen say they will press the matter at the polls if need be. The council's leisure committee has promised a decision before the year is out, and its officers have begun sketching layouts for a site beside the sports centre. The campaign's chair, retired teacher Evelyn Grieve, said the town's schoolchildren deserve better than a pool that closes every winter.
[2] Title: Baths Contract Let After Long Wait, Day: September 25, 1984 Content: The district council has let the contract for the new swimming baths, the signature falling on 24 September 1984, eight months after the design team was appointed on 8 January 1984. The council's decision to proceed had come on 17 November 1983, nine and a half months after the campaign's launch, and the pledge of a sports council grant secured the finances. The design, for a twenty-five metre pool with learner pool and sauna suite, was exhibited in the library through the spring. The tender of the chosen contractor came in four per cent under estimate. Works are expected to begin in the autumn of next year, following demolition of the laundry annexe. The campaign committee has dissolved itself with a vote of thanks to the council. The old baths will remain open until the new pool is ready, its boiler patched yet again.
[3] Title: New Baths Open at Last, Day: July 4, 1987 Content: The new swimming baths opened to the public on 3 July 1987, twenty months after work began on site on 15 October 1985. The intervening tender period, from the drawings' deposit on 6 March 1985 to the site start, had run smoothly after the delays of the design year. The opening gala, fixed for 19 September 1987, will bring the county's swimming associations to the town. The first season's admissions have beaten forecast, and the learner pool is booked solid by the schools. The sauna suite's extension, begun on 12 December 1988, was completed on 8 April 1989, adding treatment rooms let to the physiotherapy practice. The old Edwardian baths, closed at last on the eve of the opening, are to be demolished in the autumn, and their site added to the sports centre's car park. The campaign's chair, Mrs. Grieve, swam the first length at the gala.
