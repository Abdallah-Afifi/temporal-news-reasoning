[1] Title: Dial Traffic Jams on Cutover Night, Day: October 15, 1947 Content: The automatic telephone exchange came into service at eleven minutes past midnight on 14 October 1947, and within an hour the system was in trouble. Subscribers unfamiliar with the dial seized lines and let them sit, and the exchange recorded its longest queues since its installation began in March. Engineers worked through the night of 14 October 1947 and by morning had rerouted the worst-affected trunks. The old manual board, mothballed but not dismantled, was re-manned on the evening of 15 October 1947 for long-distance calls only. The telephone area has printed a quarter of a million dial instruction cards, distributed with the October bills, but the company concedes that habit will take longer than card stock. Six thousand and forty subscribers are now on the automatic equipment; the remainder transfer in stages through the spring of 1948. The exchange building itself, begun in 1939 and bricked up for the duration of the war, was finished only this summer. The manager praised the operators, four hundred and eleven of whom are being retrained as accounting and clerical staff rather than dismissed.
[2] Title: Directory Delayed as Dial Bedding In, Day: March 10, 1949 Content: The new telephone directory, delayed twice, went on sale on 8 March 1949, and with it the last of the manual exchanges in the area passed out of service on 6 March 1949. Complaints of wrong numbers, which peaked at nine hundred a day in the winter of 1947, have fallen to a hundred and ten a day, a figure the manager calls satisfactory. The retraining scheme closed on 31 December 1948 with three hundred and ninety of the original four hundred and eleven operators placed, the rest having retired or left the district. The directory itself is the first to print the letter codes for the new dial exchanges, and the printers report the compositors found the codes harder than the numbers. A memorial dinner for the manual service, held on 5 March 1949, was attended by veterans of the 1910 board. The company has announced that trunk dialling, by which a caller may dial another town without an operator, will be demonstrated at the autumn fair but is not expected for general service before the mid-1950s.
[3] Title: Automatic Exchange Vindicated by Review, Day: June 3, 1955 Content: The five-year review of the automatic telephone service, published on 1 June 1955, records that the exchange of 1947 now carries four times the calls of the manual system it replaced at two-thirds the staffing cost. The review dates the true turning point to the summer of 1949, when complaints first fell below one hundred a day. Trunk dialling arrived in the area on 18 September 1953, earlier than the cautious forecast given in 1949, and the operator's long-distance function ended entirely on 30 June 1954. The retrained operators' association, founded by the clerical transferees of 1948, marked its thousandth member this spring. The review recommends a second exchange for the eastern suburbs by 1958, the present equipment being at nine-tenths of capacity. The manager who steered the cutover, now deputy chairman, recalled the night of October 1947 at the review's launch and said the queues were the sound of the future arriving. The old manual board, kept as standby since 1947, will finally be scrapped this autumn.

[1] Title: Television Transmitter Fire Takes Channel Off Air, Day: November 21, 1957 Content: Fire broke out in the equipment room of the Woldmoor television transmitter at two o'clock on the morning of 20 November 1957, and the channel has been off air since. The fire, confined to the feeder equipment by the station's own staff before the brigade arrived, has destroyed the output stage installed new in March 1957. Viewers within twenty miles now receive nothing; those on the fringes report picking up the distant relay at strength enough for sound only. The corporation regrets the interruption and promises a service, of a kind, within days: a low-power reserve aerial on the mast's lower tier was being connected on 21 November 1957 and is expected to carry the evening programmes from tonight. Film hire for the affected region's cinemas has been arranged with the distributors' federation, and the broadcasters' varieties are to tour the larger halls. The cause of the fire is thought electrical, the feeder having run hot since the summer power increases. Programme tapes, stored clear of the blaze, are intact, and the planned Christmas productions continue in the studios.
[2] Title: Reserve Aerial Holds as Mast Repair Begins, Day: January 8, 1958 Content: The low-power reserve aerial, pressed into service on 21 November 1957, has now carried the channel for seven weeks, and viewers' letters record grudging affection for its snowy picture. The repair of the main transmitter began on 6 January 1958 with the arrival of the replacement output stage, manufactured to order and delivered by road convoy. The corporation's engineering chief expects full service to return by the end of February 1958. Meanwhile the audience has been measured for the first time since the fire: the reserve picture holds an estimated sixty per cent of the pre-fire viewership in the core area, less on the fringes. The cinema film-hire arrangement of November ended on 21 December 1957 when the halls returned to their normal programmes. The varieties tour played its last date on 14 December 1957 in the capital of the region and is not to be revived. Insurance assessors have visited the station twice; the corporation declines to state the loss. The feeder modifications ordered after the fire are to be fitted during the February shutdown for the repair itself.
[3] Title: Full Power Restored as Christmas Delayed No More, Day: March 12, 1958 Content: Full-power transmission resumed at Woldmoor at four minutes past seven on the evening of 10 March 1958, five days ahead of the February forecast, and the reserve aerial was switched off the same night. The fire's total cost, disclosed at a briefing on 11 March 1958, is put at one hundred and thirty thousand pounds, of which the output stage claimed the greater part. The Christmas productions, carried in reduced form on the reserve picture in December, will be repeated in full over the Easter holidays as compensation. The feeder modifications fitted during the repair ran cool in the first week's tests, ending the hot-running complaint of the summer of 1957. Audience measurement in the week since restoration shows the viewership back to ninety-two per cent of the pre-fire figure. The varieties tour's early dates, the corporation admits, were seen by fewer than the reserve aerial's average night. The station's staff, twenty-six at the time of the fire, is to grow by four for the coming regional opt-outs.

[1] Title: Town Orders Its First Computer, Day: September 25, 1962 Content: The council has approved the purchase of the district's first electronic computer, an accounting machine for the rates and payroll, at a meeting on 24 September 1962. The machine, to be installed in the extended wing of the treasury building, will cost sixty-eight thousand pounds and is promised for delivery in the autumn of 1963. The treasurer's report forecasts the machine will pay for itself in eight years on clerical savings alone, the rates department presently employing ninety-two clerks for the annual billing. The unions have sought and received assurances that the complement will fall only by attrition. The machine will occupy a floor load the engineers have computed at two tons, and the extension's footings were begun on 10 September 1962. A deputation of teachers was told the machine would be available, one day a month, for demonstration to senior pupils. The supplier's salesman said the district was the four hundred and eleventh local authority on the island to mechanise. Punch-card operators are to be recruited in the spring.
[2] Title: Computer Delayed as Wing Waits, Day: July 30, 1963 Content: The treasury computer, promised for the autumn, will now arrive in the new year: the supplier has written to the council blaming component allocations, and delivery is rescheduled for January 1964. The extension wing, finished in April 1963, stands empty, its humidity controls running to keep the plaster true. The punch-card operators recruited in the spring, six of them, have been trained on borrowed equipment in the county town since 3 June 1963 at the council's expense. The treasurer's office has used the delay to recode the rates system, and the coding manual reached its third draft on 15 July 1963. The deputation of teachers, renewed in May, was told the demonstration days would begin once the machine ran the payroll, now expected in the spring of 1964. Opposition members have asked whether the eight-year payback of 1962 still holds; the treasurer says it does, though the year lost will be added to the end. The machine's name, chosen by the staff competition closed on 20 June 1963, is not to be announced until installation.
[3] Title: First Rates Bills by Computer, Day: April 15, 1964 Content: The first computer-printed rates bills went out on 13 April 1964, and the treasurer reports the annual billing, which once occupied ninety-two clerks from Lady Day to midsummer, was finished in eleven working days. The machine, installed in January 1964 and named by the staff competition, first ran the payroll on 6 March 1964 for the half-month. Errors in the first billing ran at one in two hundred bills, against one in fifty in the last manual year, and the second run is expected cleaner. The teachers' demonstration days began on 24 March 1964 and are booked to the summer. The six punch-card operators, trained at the county town through 1963, now instruct the twelve new operators taken on in February. The attrition assurance of 1962 has held: the clerical complement is eighty-eight, all vacancies frozen. The supplier has offered a tape system as successor, which the treasurer's report of 31 March 1964 declines in favour of paying off the original cost first. The machine runs twenty hours a week and idles the rest, a figure the council means to improve.
