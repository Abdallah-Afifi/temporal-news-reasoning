[1] Title: Vale Co-op Opens Its First Grain Store, Day: March 30, 2017 Content: The Vale farmers' cooperative took possession of its new grain store this month, opening the doors on 5 March 2017 and dispatching its first rail shipment on 29 March 2017 to the terminal at Eastby. The store, built on the old beet pad, holds four thousand tonnes and was financed by member shares and a bank facility secured against the co-op's land. The opening completes a project that began with a members' vote at the 2016 harvest supper. Managers say the rail link removes sixty lorry journeys a week from the valley road at peak intake. The co-op's auditors have been called in for a governance review later in the year, and members will receive the scheme's first trading statement at the annual meeting. Neighbouring parishes have already asked about replicating the store on their own beet pads, and the co-op's chairman, Marl Halewood, said talks were at an early stage.
[2] Title: Vale Co-op Audit Finds Books in Order, Day: October 6, 2017 Content: The governance audit of the Vale farmers' cooperative, conducted through September 2017 at the members' insistence, found the books in order and the grain store trading ahead of forecast. The auditors' report, delivered to the committee on 3 October 2017, praises the store's intake records but recommends clearer separation of the chairman's purchasing and accounting roles. The store's irrigation permit, granted on 14 April 2017, saw the field works finished on 14 July 2017, and the auditors noted the works' accounts among the year's cleanest. The co-op confirmed that a second storage grant has been applied for, to extend the store towards the siding, though no award has yet been made and the committee declined to put a date on any announcement. The store's second intake season closed last week with sales to two maltsters and the rail contract renewed on improved terms. The audit, the first external examination in the co-op's nine-year history, cost four thousand pounds, shared between reserves and a levy of two pounds per member. The chairman welcomed the findings and said the recommended role separation would be adopted at the annual meeting.
[3] Title: Second Silo Commissioned at Vale Store, Day: April 3, 2018 Content: The Vale co-op's second grain silo, a steel bin of two thousand tonnes beside the original store, was commissioned on 12 March 2018 in a short ceremony attended by the parish council. The silo, ordered after the success of the first trading year, allows separate storage of malting and feed grades. Engineering inspectors signed the fire and dust certification in the same week, and the co-op's insurers have reduced the premium accordingly. Intake for the coming harvest is forecast to double, and the committee has approved a second shift of storekeepers. The auditors' recommendation on role separation, accepted at the annual meeting held on 3 April 2018, took effect with the new committee elected that evening. The second storage grant applied for last autumn remains undetermined, the funding body having deferred its decision cycle. The store's first specialist oat intake for members ran from 7 July to 31 July 2017, and the egg pool's winter round followed from 3 January to 27 January 2018. Managers expect the rail contract to be renegotiated once the doubled intake is demonstrated through a full season.

[1] Title: Mill Resumes Grinding After Stone Dressing, Day: September 8, 2016 Content: The water mill at Threshford resumed grinding on 6 September 2016 after its millwright completed the stone dressing begun on 14 August 2016. The mill, which had turned commercially since the 1970s on organic grists, was stopped for the work after the bedstone showed ridging. The millwright, one of four in the county still working burr stone, re-cut the furrows and re-balanced the runner. The interruption is the mill's longest in a decade and its customers, mostly artisan bakers, were supplied from reserve sacks milled before the stoppage. The miller says output should return to normal with the new wheat crop. The mill's flour campaign for the season had begun on 12 June 2016 and the first pallets of the new season's flour left the door on 30 June 2016, both before the stoppage. An open day is planned for the autumn to show the dressed stones to customers.
[2] Title: Feed Mill Certified Ahead of Schedule, Day: September 9, 2016 Content: The compound feed mill at Droneford received its final feed-hygiene certification on 7 September 2016, ahead of the date its owners had projected. The certification follows the installation of the new batching plant and clears the mill to supply co-op members with winter rations. The mill's managers thanked the inspectors for a schedule kept through the summer. The mill processes home-grown pulses and bought-in soya, and its certification was the last obstacle to the co-op's feed scheme, which has enrolled ninety holdings since spring. A open evening for members is fixed for later in the month, and the mill's old pelleting line, replaced this year, will be sold at auction in the autumn. The mill's laboratory has been accredited separately since the spring, and its mycotoxin screening is now accepted by both maltsters in the county town.
[3] Title: Orchard's Long Harvest Ends, Day: December 23, 2015 Content: The last bin of the season's apples was hauled out of the Duncrow orchard on 21 December 2015, nineteen weeks after picking began on 3 October 2015, bringing to a close the longest harvest in the orchard's recorded history. The season's length reflects the new late varieties planted to extend sales past the solstice. The orchard's cheese-making neighbours at Rowbank reported a strong year, their cave batch started on 9 February 2015 having been released on 17 May 2015 after its full aging, and the dairy's ledger shows the batch sold out within a fortnight of release. The orchard's own juice press ran until the eve of the solstice. Buyers from the city market took the late fruit, and the orchard reports that storage losses were below two per cent, attributed to the new controlled-atmosphere store built last year. Pruning gangs begin in the first week of the new year.

[1] Title: Hop Harvest Starts Early, Day: September 10, 2018 Content: The Kilburn hop gardens began picking on 8 September 2018, a week earlier than the customary date, and the oast's kiln will close for the season on 2 October 2018 if the forecast holds. The early start follows a warm August that brought the cones to condition ahead of the calendar. Pickers, mostly students and travelling gangs, have filled the first bins of the variety Early Bird. The grower's association says quality is above the five-year mean. The kiln's refurbished dryer, installed last winter, will be tested at full load for the first time this season. Brewers from three counties have contracted for the whole crop in advance, an arrangement the association's secretary called unprecedented in living memory. The asparagus beds on the garden's lower terrace, cut from 25 April to 12 July this year, are being let to rest until the next moon. A picking supper is planned for the closing weekend, and the association's annual general meeting follows a fortnight later at the Green Man.
[2] Title: Dairy Pool Formed in Marsdale, Day: February 1, 2019 Content: A new dairy pool was formally constituted on 29 January 2019 by thirty-two producers of the Marsdale vale, pooling their milk contracting to gain standing with the regional processor. The pool's rules were adopted at a meeting in the mart hall, and its first board was elected the same evening. The pool is the second such venture in the county; the first, among the cheese makers of the west vale, has traded since the millennium. Members' contracts transfer at the next collection. The high pasture farms' new lambing barn, begun on 4 June 2018, was pronounced ready on 4 December 2018 by the same firm of joiners. The pool has applied for start-up funding from the rural programme, and its chairman, Gwen Paskell, said the pool's first test will be the spring flush. The mart's weekly ring has reported record throughputs of store cattle this month, and the pool's formation was greeted by the processor with a statement of cautious welcome. The pool's offices open in the old creamery building on Monday.
[3] Title: Market Committee Marks Its New Site, Day: June 24, 2019 Content: The permanent site of the Wetherby farmers' market opened on 23 June 2019, three years after the committee that conceived it first met on 23 May 2016 in the back room of the seed merchants. The site, a paved yard with permanent stalls and cold storage, was built with council support and traders' shares. The market had traded in the car park of the civic hall through the intervening years, come rain and fair. Saturday's opening was marked by the ringing of the old corn exchange bell, rescued from the demolished exchange and mounted at the yard's entrance. The committee's chair, retired grower Aldous Finch, said the achievement belonged to the forty founding traders. The market's subscription vegetable scheme, its sister venture, continues from the yard's new packing room, having been launched on 15 September 2015 and reached its membership capacity on 30 November 2015. The seed library that shares the yard's office, which opened its drawers on 2 February 2016, recorded its ten-thousandth loan on 2 February 2019. The old cider press house, destroyed by fire on 10 October 2017, saw its replacement open on 10 March 2019, and the yard's demonstration garden opens to schools in the autumn. The vale's mobile dairy clinic, which visited the Rowbank dairy on 18 March 2016 and returned on 18 June 2016, now calls quarterly at the yard. An earlier seed fair had run from 20 January to 15 February 2015 in the corn exchange, and it was there that the committee's founding traders first met. The Duncrow vineyard's first commercial release, which came on 11 November 2018 three years to the day after its planting on 11 November 2015, was toasted at Saturday's opening. Trading continues each Saturday from dawn.
