[1] Title: Bank Lifts Rates Off the Floor, Day: December 16, 2021 Content: The central bank raised its policy rate on 15 December 2021 by fifteen basis points, the first increase in eleven years, taking the rate off the zero floor adopted in March 2020. The governor cited inflation at 4.8 per cent, more than twice the target, and a labour market described as tighter than any since the surveys began. Two of the nine committee members voted for a larger move; one voted for no change. The statement dropped the word "temporary" from its description of price pressures, a deletion the markets read within minutes. The currency strengthened half a per cent on the announcement. Savers' groups welcomed the move while noting that deposit rates had yet to follow; the banks' association said lending margins would absorb the first steps. The governor's press conference ran long, the first question on the committee's own forecast miss — inflation projected at 2.3 per cent for this quarter a year ago. The next meeting falls on 3 February 2022.
[2] Title: Half-Point Steps as Inflation Bites, Day: June 15, 2022 Content: The committee raised the policy rate by half a point on 15 June 2022, the third such increase since April, bringing the rate to 1.75 per cent, its highest since 2009. Inflation was reported at 8.2 per cent for May, and the bank's staff now see it peaking above eleven in the autumn. The decisions of December 2021 and February 2022 look, in the minutes, like the toe-in-the-water they were; the March 2022 meeting, which began the half-point sequence, also launched the wind-down of the bond portfolio accumulated under the zero-rate years. Mortgage lenders passed the June rise through within a day, and the five-year fixed rate crossed four per cent. The unions' federation wrote to the governor calling the sequence a tax on borrowers; he replied that inflation was the deeper tax. The committee's vote was seven to two, the minority wanting three-quarters. The governor, asked in parliament on 8 June 2022 whether the bank had fallen behind, said the committee would do what was necessary, and declined the word "behind".
[3] Title: Pause Declared as Prices Turn, Day: August 9, 2023 Content: The committee held the policy rate at 5.25 per cent on 8 August 2023, the sixth consecutive hold since the increases ended in September 2022, and the governor said the question before the committee was now the length of the plateau, not its height. Inflation stood at 6.1 per cent in June, down from the 11.4 per cent peak of October 2022 but nearer the top of the bank's own fan chart than its centre. The bond portfolio wind-down, running since March 2022, passed its planned halfway mark this month. The half-point sequence of 2022 has passed into the textbooks' rough drafts: December 2021's fifteen points, then twenty-five, then the half-points, then the three-quarter leap of November 2022 that took the rate to its plateau. The savers' groups, quiet through the zero years, now protest the deposit spreads. The committee's minutes record a debate on the plateau's language, "sufficiently restrictive" surviving an amendment. The next meeting's date is set for 20 September 2023.

=== PASSAGE P2 ===
[1] Title: Depositors Queue as Regional Bank Wobbles, Day: March 10, 2023 Content: Customers of the Corvus Bank queued at branches and hammered its app through the morning of 10 March 2023, withdrawing what the bank later confirmed as a third of its deposits in a single day. The run followed the previous day's disclosure of a capital raise abandoned mid-sale and a paper loss on bonds bought in the zero-rate years. The bank's chairman resigned at noon; by the close the shares had been halted twice. The regulator placed Corvus under special administration at six in the evening of 10 March 2023, citing liquidity, not solvency, in its first statement. Payroll customers, some eleven thousand firms, faced a weekend of uncertainty over the month's wages. The finance ministry convened its crisis committee, and by night the chancellor was promising that depositors would be able to draw on Monday. Corvus's loan book, concentrated in the region's wine trade and small manufacturers, was said by insiders to be sound. The bank had 190,000 depositors and forty-one branches at the close of its last reporting year.
[2] Title: Deposits Guaranteed, Buyer Found for Corvus, Day: March 22, 2023 Content: The sale of Corvus Bank to the Regional Consolidated group was agreed in the small hours of 21 March 2023, eleven days after the run, with all deposits and the forty-one branches transferring at the open of business. The guarantee that preceded the sale — depositors made whole from Monday 13 March 2023, as promised — has now been folded into the purchase, and the special administration ends. The bondholders' association has signalled challenge in the courts, its members facing losses under the resolution rules; the ministry says the rules were applied as written. Payroll customers were paid over the weekend of 15-16 March 2023 without gap. The wine trade's cooperatives, meeting on 18 March 2023, thanked the regulator's speed and noted their credit lines had not been called. The chairman who resigned at noon on the run's day has accepted a summons to the treasury committee for 27 March 2023. Regional Consolidated's chief called Corvus a well-run bank unhorsed by its bond book, a phrase that traveled.
[3] Title: Post-Mortem Rules Speed Was the Exception, Day: January 17, 2024 Content: The review of the Corvus failure, published on 16 January 2024, finds the run of 10 March 2023 to have been the fastest in the jurisdiction's recorded history — a third of deposits in a day, where the classic runs took weeks. The review credits the guarantee of 13 March and the sale of 21 March with halting contagion: no other institution lost more than two per cent of deposits that week. It faults the bank's own risk office, whose 2021 memo on the bond book's rate sensitivity was answered with a shrug in writing. The treasury committee's sessions of March and April 2023 are cited; the chairman's evidence, given on 27 March 2023, is described as candid to the point of self-destruction. The review recommends deposit coverage be reviewed annually against concentration, the first such review due by June 2024. The bondholders' challenge failed in the administrative court on 30 November 2023. Regional Consolidated has kept all forty-one branches and reports Corvus's wine clients intact. The regulator's chair called the episode the system working, and added that the system should not be asked to work that way again.

=== PASSAGE P3 ===
[1] Title: Inflation Halves as Energy Anchor Drops, Day: August 15, 2023 Content: The consumer price index for July 2023, published on 15 August 2023, showed inflation at 5.2 per cent, less than half the 11.4 per cent of October 2022, and the lowest reading since the autumn of 2021. The fall was led by energy, the wholesale markets having released the war premium, and by food, whose deceleration the statistics office called broad. Core inflation, the committee's watched measure, fell more grudgingly, from 6.4 to 5.9 per cent. Wage settlements in the second quarter averaged 5.7 per cent, and the bank's agents reported the first mentions of below-inflation deals in the engineering trades since the cycle began. The governor, speaking after the statistics release, repeated that the plateau of rates — unchanged since September 2022 at 5.25 per cent — would be held until the job was done. Markets priced a first cut for the spring of 2024. The unions' federation welcomed the direction and noted members' mortgages were written at 2022's rates.
[2] Title: First Cut Lands as Target Comes Into View, Day: June 19, 2024 Content: The committee cut the policy rate by a quarter point on 18 June 2024, the first reduction since the cycle of increases ended in September 2022, taking the rate to 5.00 per cent. Inflation was 2.6 per cent in May, and the committee's statement described the return to target as in sight rather than achieved. The vote was six to three, the minority preferring to wait a further meeting; the minutes record the governor in the majority. The cut was dated by the markets within a minute and priced as the first of three before the year's end. Mortgage holders on the standard variable rate saw the pass-through announced the same afternoon. The savers' groups, who had tasted 5 per cent for the first time in fifteen years, called the cut premature; the unions' federation called it overdue and pointed to the wage settlements, now averaging 4.1 per cent. The bank's bond portfolio, run down since March 2022, is a third smaller than its peak. The next meeting falls on 7 August 2024.
[3] Title: Target Hit at Last, Says Statistics Office, Day: October 16, 2024 Content: The consumer price index for September 2024, published on 16 October 2024, showed inflation at the 2 per cent target for the first time since March 2021 — forty-two months above it, a stretch the office compares only with the 1970s. The governor marked the moment in a lecture on 17 October 2024, crediting the plateau held from August 2023, the second cut of August 2024 and the third of September 2024, which took the rate to 4.5 per cent. The committee's own minutes, published the same week, describe the easing path as gradual and conditional. Wage settlements averaged 3.4 per cent in the third quarter, the engineering trades' below-inflation deals of 2023 having spread. The mortgage reset wave of 2024-25, borrower advocacy groups note, is still to crest, and they asked the committee to remember it; the minutes record the point made by an external member. The bond portfolio's run-down is complete as of 30 September 2024. The savers' groups have opened their campaign for the next cycle. The next meeting is set for 6 November 2024.
