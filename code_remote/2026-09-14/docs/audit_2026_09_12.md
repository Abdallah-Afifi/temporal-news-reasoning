## 0a. CRITICAL (found on the fix pass) — 64% of TRAM was evaluated on prompts that omitted the question

`BenchmarkLoader._tram_csv_to_example` dropped the column that carries the
actual problem for three TRAM tasks. The code was:

```python
question = row.get("Question", "").strip()
if not question:                       # never true
    premise = row.get("Premise", "")
    if premise:
        question = row.get("Hypothesis", "").strip()
```

Every TRAM NLI row **has** a `Question` column — but it holds a constant
instruction ("Do the hypothesis and premise logically entail each other,
contradict each other, or remain undetermined?"). The fallback therefore never
fired and the **`Hypothesis` never reached the prompt**. Three consecutive rows
sharing a premise produced *byte-identical* prompts with different golds:

```
Context: An older man sits with his orange juice at a small table …
Question: Do the hypothesis and premise logically entail each other, …?
Choices: A. entailment  B. neutral  C. contradiction
```
→ golds `neutral`, `contradiction`, `neutral`.

Two more instances of the same class:

- **`storytelling_mcq`** (67,204 items) keeps its passage in a `Story` column
  that was never read, while the question asks *"Which of the two endings is
  the most plausible correct ending to the story?"*.
- **`context`** fell back to the `Source` column, so `relation_*` (204,914
  items) rendered `Context: TempEval-3` — a dataset name — as the passage.

### How large

| affected | items | share of TRAM |
|---|---|---|
| `nli_mcq` + `nli_saq` — hypothesis missing | 564,268 | 57.5% |
| `storytelling_mcq` — story missing | 67,204 | 6.9% |
| `relation_*` — provenance string as context | 204,914 | 20.9% |
| **total whose prompts change under the fix** | **836,386** | **85.3%** |

The fingerprint was visible in the project's own tooling and never followed
up: `scripts/audit_tram.py` has been printing *"internal exact
(question,answer) duplicates: 736,854"* — 75% of the benchmark — every time it
ran. Measured per task before the fix, `nli_mcq` and `nli_saq` had **3 unique
(question, answer) pairs each** across 282,134 items. And every arm scored
30.2–34.6% on a **three-way** task whose chance rate is 33.3%.

### The fix, and how it checks out

`src/data/data_loader.py` now sets `question = Hypothesis` when one is present
(the contract `_nli_instruction` in `run_baselines.py` already assumed), takes
`context` from `Premise or Story`, and never uses `Source`.

Independent confirmation: after the fix TRAM contains **526,454 unique
(context, question, answer) triples**. The TRAM paper documents **526,668
problems**. Before the fix the same count was 244,064. The loader now recovers
the benchmark's own documented problem count to within 0.04%.

Per-task duplication after the fix: `nli_mcq` 0.0%, `nli_saq` 0.0%,
`storytelling` 0.0% (were 100%, 100%, 0.4%). The residual 50% in
relation/ordering/arithmetic/typical_time/duration/frequency is the genuine
MCQ+SAQ format pairing described in §3.2 — the same problem asked twice.

### Live confirmation from the re-run (2026-09-12 15:0x)

First NLI rows off the fixed-prompt zero-shot leg, 4,368 of 282,134:

| | before the fix | after |
|---|---|---|
| unique questions in the NLI block | **1** | 4,343 of 4,368 |
| zero-shot EM on NLI | 34.6% (chance = 33.3%) | **50.5%** |

shift roughly holds, zero-shot's TRAM micro-average rises ~9pp from the
published 35.20%, and every arm moves by its own amount. That is why the whole
column is re-run rather than patched.

most of all) keep it. So the mild intervention is not the cheapest — it is the
most format-destructive, because pure span extraction is furthest from
classification. That is a mechanistic result, and it replaces reading #4.

## 0a-bis. The finding the pooling was hiding — fine-tuning helps on gold context and hurts on retrieved context

Found while sweeping TIME and TimeBench for the §0a bug class (they are clean —
see below). TIME ships a `Setting` field that the loader discards and that
appears **nowhere** in this project's code or docs:

| Setting | rows | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| `base` | 42,380 (40.4%) | the gold context |
| `bm25` | 20,857 (19.9%) | context supplied by BM25 retrieval |
| `vector` | 20,857 (19.9%) | context supplied by dense retrieval |
| `hybrid` | 20,857 (19.9%) | context supplied by hybrid retrieval |

So **every TIME number in this project is a pooled average over four retrieval
conditions**, and the conditions are ~23pp apart (zero-shot: 27.4% on `base`,
~50-52% on the retrieved settings). Stratifying — with the §1.1 abstain
artifact excluded, so this is the clean comparison:

| arm | base (n=42,173) | bm25 (20,113) | vector (20,113) | hybrid (20,113) | pooled |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| zero-shot | 27.21% | 50.07% | 51.19% | 51.62% | 41.19% |
| v6 | **29.40%** | 48.34% | 48.85% | 49.18% | 40.81% |
| v7c | **29.97%** | 47.13% | 48.11% | 48.62% | 40.56% |

| Δ vs zero-shot | base | bm25 | vector | hybrid | pooled |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v6 | **+2.18** | −1.73 | −2.33 | −2.44 | −0.38 |
| v7c | **+2.76** | −2.94 | −3.08 | −3.00 | −0.64 |

Paired significance (McNemar, abstain items excluded):

| comparison | n | Δ | z |
|---|---|---|---|
| v6 vs zs, `base` | 42,173 | +2.18pp | **+11.6** |
| v6 vs zs, retrieved | 60,339 | −2.17pp | **−11.2** |
| v7c vs zs, `base` | 42,173 | +2.76pp | **+14.5** |
| v7c vs zs, retrieved | 60,339 | −3.01pp | **−16.0** |

Both effects are large, consistent across all three retrievers independently,
and present in both headline arms. They are not noise, and they cancel:

> **The campaign's "fine-tuning does not help" conclusion is a pooling
> artifact. Fine-tuning delivers a real, significant +2.2 to +2.8pp gain on
> the 40% of TIME with gold context, and a real, significant −2.2 to −3.0pp
> loss on the 60% with retrieved context. Averaging the two reports ≈ 0.**

This is a mechanistic result, it is directly on the thesis's own subject
(temporal-aware RAG), and it is considerably stronger than either "v6 beats
zero-shot by +0.73pp" (which §1.1 dissolves) or "nothing worked". The natural
reading: fine-tuning on clean Wikipedia-style QA sharpens the model's use of a
clean context and makes it *less* robust to the noisy, partially-irrelevant
passages a retriever returns — which is exactly the condition the deployed
system would run in.

**Report TIME stratified by `Setting` from here on.** Pooling four conditions
that differ by 23pp, and in which the treatment effect changes sign, is not a
summary — it is a cancellation.

### The TRAM re-run is written and ready to launch

`scripts/run_schedule_tram_rerun.sh` — ten arms (zero-shot + v1, v2, v3c, v4,
v5, v6, v7, v7c, v6d), vLLM, on the fixed prompts. Protocol unchanged in every
other respect: same engine, greedy, seed 42, `max_new_tokens` 128, same
postprocessing and scorer. No training happens; it reuses the trained
adapters.

- **Output goes to a NEW root**, `results/tram_fixed/<arm>/`. It deliberately
  does not write into `results/corrected/<arm>_vllm/`: those hold the stale
  predictions, their `model_dir`/`adapter_dir` tags would MATCH, and the
  runner would resume and append new predictions onto old ones — blending two
  prompt regimes inside one arm. The stale files stay as the record.
- **A precheck aborts the run** if the loader fix is not active: it asserts
  TRAM yields >500,000 unique (context, question, answer) triples. Before the
  fix that number was 244,064, so a stale checkout cannot silently regenerate
  the broken prompts. Verified passing: 980,918 items / 526,467 unique.
- **Disk-safe**: five arms (v1, v2, v3c, v4, v5) have no merged model. The
  schedule merges → evaluates → deletes each merge it creates, keeping peak
  extra usage near 6 GB plus ~650 MB of predictions per arm. Merges that
  already existed (v6, v6d, v7, v7c) are reused and never deleted. It aborts
  before any merge if free space is under 12 GB (currently 27 GB free).
- **Resumable and fail-fast**: per-stage `.done` markers, `VLLM_USE_FLASHINFER_SAMPLER=0`
  (D55), the conditional completion marker, and the new exclusive lock (B13)
  so a double-launch is refused rather than interleaved.
- `scripts/rescore_v5_protocol.py --tram-root results/tram_fixed` scores the
  result into `results/rescored/v5_protocol_tram_fixed.json`; TIME and
  TimeBench are untouched and keep reproducing their published numbers.

**Cost: ~2h15 per arm (the fixed prompts are longer than the 127 ex/s measured
on the broken ones), so ~22–24 h for ten arms, plus ~4 min CPU per merge and
~10 min for the rescore.**

**It must be launched from a shell with real GPU access.** This Claude Code
session has none — re-confirmed on 2026-09-12: `/dev/nvidia0` exists but
`nvidia-smi` is absent from PATH, `/proc/driver/nvidia/version` is unreadable

this boundary does not lift with the tool's sandbox flag disabled, and that a

```

nohup bash scripts/run_schedule_tram_rerun.sh > logs/sched_tram_rerun/driver.log 2>&1 &
```

| item | measured impact | verdict |

## 0c. Verdict in one paragraph

The engineering discipline of this campaign is unusually good: raw predictions
are immutable and all scoring is recomputed from them, arm configs differ by
exactly the lines they claim to, guards are real and enforced by asserts, and
the canonical table reproduces bit-for-bit. Two things are nonetheless wrong
at the level of the results. **First (§0a): 85.3% of the TRAM column was
generated from prompts that omitted the question** — the NLI hypothesis and
the storytelling passage never reached the model — so that benchmark must be
re-run, and on the part that *was* validly measured its headline conclusion
reverses. **Second, the methodology has one load-bearing problem**: the campaign's only positive result — "a fine-tuned arm
beats zero-shot on TIME" — does not survive removal of a benchmark artifact the
project itself discovered and documented on 2026-09-08 (D49), and the campaign
reference document written three days later (`docs/results_and_methodology.md`,
D62) drops that caveat and extends the headline to an arm (v7c) the caveat was
never applied to. Once the artifact is removed, **no fine-tuned arm beats
zero-shot on any of the three benchmarks.** That is still a publishable result —
it is a clean, well-evidenced negative result about specialisation — but it is
not the result currently written down.

## 1. CRITICAL — the headline claim does not survive the abstain artifact

### 1.1 The artifact, measured

2,427 TIME items present an abstain-phrased option ("There is no answer") among
their choices. In **2,427 of 2,427** the abstain option is the gold. A model
that simply picks the abstain option whenever one is offered scores **100% with
no temporal reasoning at all**.

| arm | TIME overall | abstain-option items (n=2,427) | rest of TIME (n=102,512) |
|---|---|---|---|
| zero-shot | 41.46% | 52.95% | **41.19%** |
| v6 | 42.18% | 99.88% | **40.81%** (−0.38 vs zs) |
| v7c | 41.91% | 99.18% | **40.56%** (−0.63 vs zs) |
| v6d | 39.96% | 97.20% | 38.61% |
| v3-corr | 39.98% | 7.21% | 40.75% |

So the entire +0.72pp (v6) and +0.45pp (v7c) margin, and more, comes from this
one bucket. On the other 97.7% of TIME every fine-tuned arm is **below**
zero-shot. Combined with TimeBench (best FT arm +3.28pp, but see §4) and TRAM
(no FT arm above zero-shot on the micro-average), the campaign's honest
top-line is:

> **No fine-tuned arm in the campaign beats the zero-shot baseline on any
> benchmark once the abstain artifact is excluded.**

### 1.2 Why this is an audit finding and not a repeat of D49

D49 (2026-09-08) found this for v6, quantified it as "strip it and v6 is 41.10
vs 41.46", and said in bold: *"Do not report +0.73pp without this."* That was
the right call. But:

- `docs/results_and_methodology.md` (2026-09-11), which declares itself "the
  single reference for … how the numbers are scored", contains **no mention of
  the abstain artifact**. Its §3 v6 chapter carries only the `Order_Compare`
  caveat (D56); its v6 headline row is bolded as "the first and only arm to
  beat zero-shot".
- The caveat was **never applied to v7c at all**, which that document promotes
  to "the methodologically clean headline arm". v7c depends on the artifact for
  a larger share of its margin than v6 does (99.18% of a +0.45pp margin).

**Fix:** add an artifact-excluded column to the canonical table, regenerate it
inside `rescore_v5_protocol.py` (the item set is derivable — "any item whose
choices contain an abstain phrase"), and restate the campaign verdict.

Repro:
```bash
# ids: any TIME example whose choices contain "no answer"/"unanswerable"
# then score each arm on the complement
```

### 1.3 The one gain that *is* real

The largest single category movement in the project — `Counterfactual`
42.6% → 55.7% — is **not** a shortcut, and it deserves to be separated from the
artifact it is currently bundled with. Checked three ways on its MCQ half
(n=5,970, where zero-shot 62.40% → v6 79.92%):

- gold positions are balanced (A/B/C/D = 1,444 / 1,556 / 1,440 / 1,530), so
  there is no majority option to exploit;
- "pick the longest option" is only 26.9% accurate, and v6 agrees with it
  **less** than zero-shot does (24.1% vs 32.1%);
- the mechanism is **position-bias reduction**: zero-shot over-picks A
  (1,749 A vs 1,100 D); v6 is nearly flat (1,522 / 1,697 / 1,486 / 1,262).

That is a genuine, reportable fine-tuning effect and currently the strongest
positive finding in the campaign. It is worth its own paragraph rather than a
line inside a bucket table.

### 1.4 `AUG_NOANS` failed on its own target — the opposite of what the docs credit

Free-text TIME items whose gold *is* "There is no answer" (n=2,532, spread over
Counterfactual 1,110 / Relative_Reasoning 904 / Co_temporality 518) score
**0.00% for every arm**, including the ones trained to abstain. Meanwhile
`AUG_NOANS` roughly doubled the *false* abstention rate:

| arm | emits "there is no answer" | of those, correct |
|---|---|---|
| zero-shot | 1,291 (1.23%) | 0 |
| v6 | 2,424 (2.31%) | 0 |
| v7c | 2,407 (2.29%) | 0 |

`AUG_NOANS` bought exactly one thing: near-100% on the 2,427 items where the
abstain text is a *listed option* (§1.1). Everywhere else it cost ~1,100 extra
wrong answers. The v6 chapter should say this; today it lists AUG_NOANS as part
of the winning bundle.

## 2. HIGH — "0 scorer-rule firings" is a misread counter

`docs/results_and_methodology.md` §1 credits v7c with "0 scorer-rule firings",
and D49 credits v6 with "+0.86pp with 0 postprocessor rewrites". Both come from
the `rewritten` field in `rescore_v5_protocol.rescore()`, which counts
`_postprocess_prediction(raw) != r["prediction"]` — i.e. **disagreement between
the postprocessor now and the one that ran at generation time**. It is a
protocol-drift counter, not a rule-firing counter. Arms generated after the
postprocessor froze necessarily read 0.

Measured properly (postprocessed prediction vs the bare first line of the raw
output, TIME):

| arm | rules actually fire | accuracy, rules on | accuracy, first line only | **credit from the extraction rules** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| zero-shot | 49.4% of items | 41.46% | 40.49% | **+1.00pp** |
| v6 | 49.3% | 42.18% | 29.22% | **+12.97pp** |
| v7c | 16.9% | 41.91% | 41.70% | **+0.21pp** |

Two consequences:

1. **v6 must not be described as "already answers in the benchmark's expected
   form."** Under a strict first-line rule v6 scores 29.22% against zero-shot's
   40.49% — a 12.97pp differential dependency on the answer-extraction layer,
   which is exactly the "you trained a nonstandard format, then widened the
   scorer" objection.
2. **The v7c argument is stronger than the document actually makes it.** v7c
   leads zero-shot under *both* protocols (41.91 vs 41.46 with rules; 41.70 vs
   40.49 without). That is the real, defensible form of the claim — and it
   should be made with this measurement, not with the `rewritten` counter.

**Fix:** rename the field (`protocol_drift`), and add a genuine
`rules_fired` / `strict_first_line_pct` pair to the rescore output.

## 3. HIGH — the statistics under-use the paired design and over-count n

### 3.1 Wrong test, conservatively wrong

`rescore_v5_protocol.ztest` is an **unpaired two-proportion z-test** applied to
**paired** data (identical items, different arms). It ignores the ~80% of items
on which two arms agree, inflating the standard error. `scripts/mcnemar_v5_protocol.py`
exists and implements the correct test, but the canonical document quotes the
unpaired numbers:

| comparison (TIME) | Δ | project z (unpaired) | McNemar z (paired) |
|---|---|---|---|
| v6 vs zero-shot | +0.72pp | +3.32 | **+5.18** (b=10,145 c=10,896) |
| v7c vs zero-shot | +0.45pp | +2.09 | **+3.30** (b=9,979 c=10,450) |

The error direction is conservative, so no significance call flips. Quote
McNemar anyway — the tooling is already there.

### 3.2 The denominators are not independent trials

Both tests assume n independent Bernoulli trials. They are not:

- **TIME** — 104,939 items over **49,316 unique contexts** and **56,229 unique
  question strings**. One question string appears 11,361 times (the Timeline
  template); one context appears 1,800 times. 48,710 items are duplicate
  question strings.
- **TRAM** — 980,918 items are ~465k underlying problems counted **twice**, once
  as MCQ and once as SAQ: `arithmetic_mcq` 15,584 / `arithmetic_saq` 15,584;
  `nli_mcq` 282,134 / `nli_saq` 282,134; `relation_mcq` 102,457 /
  `relation_saq` 102,457; and so on for duration, frequency, ordering,
  typical_time. The loader correctly excludes the `*_shots_*` few-shot files,
  but nothing dedupes the format pair.

At these n everything is "significant"; the phrase "all differences significant
at n = 980,918" in §9 of the reference document is not informative. Report
cluster-robust intervals (cluster on context for TIME, on problem for TRAM), or
report per-task results and stop aggregating.

### 3.3 No variance estimate exists anywhere

`docs/audit_report.md:82` flagged "Single seed, greedy-only, no confidence
intervals anywhere" and it was never closed. There is **one training run per
arm, one seed (42), no replicate**. The campaign's decisive margins are
+0.72pp and +0.45pp. Nothing in the repository establishes that those exceed
run-to-run variance. One seed-43 replicate of v6 (~8 h) would either rescue or
kill the headline, and is the highest-value GPU-hour left in the project.

### 3.4 The benchmarks were used as a development set

Ten arms were designed, trained and selected against the same TIME/TimeBench
test sets, and the reported headline is the maximum over those ten. There is no
held-out confirmation split. Combined with §3.3 this is the standard
multiple-comparisons hazard, and it is the reason §1.1 matters so much: a
+0.45pp max-of-ten margin that evaporates when one artifact bucket is removed is
not a robust finding.

## 4. HIGH — two of the three headline conclusions are artifacts of micro-averaging

### 4.1 TRAM's conclusion reverses under macro-average

TRAM is **57.5% `temporal_nli` and 20.9% `temporal_relation`**. The micro-average
is therefore close to "NLI accuracy". Averaging over the ten tasks instead:

| | zero-shot | v4 | v7c | v6d | v6 | v3-corr |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TRAM micro (as published) | **35.20** | 32.01 | 32.00 | 31.56 | 31.15 | 30.18 |
| TRAM macro over 10 tasks | 44.98 | **45.09** | 44.40 | 43.71 | 43.49 | 42.63 |

Reading #1 of §9 — *"No fine-tuned arm beats zero-shot on TRAM"* — holds only
under micro-averaging. Under macro-averaging **v4 beats zero-shot**. Neither
choice is wrong, but the conclusion is presented as a property of the models
when it is a property of the aggregation. State the choice, show both, or
report per task.

### 4.2 TIME's and TimeBench's gains live in two categories each

Decomposing each arm's overall delta vs zero-shot into per-category
contributions (pp of the total):

**TIME, v6's +0.72pp:** Counterfactual **+1.21**, Order_Compare **+0.72**,
Localization +0.51, Timeline +0.17 … against Duration_Compare −0.64,
Computation −0.57, Relative_Reasoning −0.21. Nine of eleven categories are flat
or negative.

**TimeBench, v6's +2.26pp:** `arithmetic` alone contributes **+3.81**; every
other category nets −1.55. `arithmetic` is precisely the category `AUG_ARITH`
targets.

The honest framing is: **fine-tuning moves the categories the synthetic slices
directly target, and costs a little everywhere else.** That is a clean,
defensible thesis statement and it is better supported than the current one.

### 4.3 Several large categories are at or below chance for every arm

| category | n | %TIME | random | zero-shot | v6 | v7c |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Timeline | 13,159 | 12.5% | **14.4%** (mostly 3 facts → 1/6) | 16.5% | 17.9% | 18.8% |
| Duration_Compare | 9,217 | 8.8% | **33.3%** | 40.4% | **33.1%** | 41.4% |
| Computation | 9,372 | 8.9% | ~0% (free text) | 18.8% | 12.5% | **1.8%** |

v6 is exactly at chance on Duration_Compare; v7c has effectively lost

Side note: the project's global "random baseline 13.56%" should be rechecked —
it appears to assume 1/n-choices per item, which is wrong for `Timeline`

6.7%, not 25%). `Extract`'s 7–10% is therefore slightly **above** chance, not
far below it.

## 9. What verified clean (and should be said in the thesis)

These were checked adversarially and hold:

- **Config discipline is real.** `diff config_v6.yaml config_v7_corrected.yaml`
  → exactly 3 lines (two data paths + output dir). The "data only, one variable"
  claim is true as stated.
- **Immutable predictions + CPU rescoring works.** `rescore_v5_protocol.py`
  reproduces the published table exactly from raw predictions; protocol changes
  really are applied uniformly to every arm. This is the single best design
  decision in the project and it is what made this audit possible.
- **Engine parity is *better* than documented.** Measured v6 HF vs vLLM
